Charades for Kids – 60 Easy Words for Classroom & Playdates

🎭

Written by the Charades Generator team. We build the free word generator this site runs on. The last time we went through the word bank by hand we cut sixty-three entries — words that read perfectly well on a list and died the moment somebody actually had to act one out. That is the bar everything here is held to.

Charades needs no materials and no setup, which is why it survives in classrooms long after the craft supplies have run out. What it does need is words matched to the age of the child holding the card. Give a five-year-old "Photosynthesis" and you have not set a challenge, you have set them up to stand at the front of a room doing nothing. The 60 words below are grouped by age for exactly that reason.

If you would rather not hold a list while managing a room, the Kids Charades Generator picks age-appropriate words one at a time with a timer built in.

Ages 4–6: Toddler & Pre-K Words

Four to six is the age where the word has to be something the child has personally done or personally seen. Animals and everyday actions, nothing metaphorical. Let the sound effects go — enforcing silence with this age group costs you more than it buys, and a roaring lion is the whole point of a roaring lion.

How to Run Charades in a Classroom

A classroom is a different problem from a party, and the difference is not the children — it is the ratio. Thirty of them and one turn at a time means twenty-nine are watching, and twenty-nine bored children is not a lull, it is a discipline problem. Everything below is aimed at keeping the non-acting majority busy:

1️⃣
Split into two teams
Teams of 4–6 work best. Too small and you run out of guessers, too large and kids disengage. Mixed ages work well — younger kids can guess even if they can't act yet.
2️⃣
Use a visible timer
Project a countdown on the board, or use a loud kitchen timer. Kids love the urgency — it keeps everyone watching even when it's not their turn.
3️⃣
Write words on cards in advance
Prep 20–30 cards before the session. Keep two separate decks — easier cards for younger kids, harder ones for older groups. Draw from the appropriate deck per actor.
4️⃣
Rotate actors every round
Every child should act at least once. Go around the room in order so no one gets skipped. Kids who are shy often surprise everyone once they're up.

Kid-Friendly Rule Adjustments

The standard rules were written for adults who chose to be there. A few of them work against young players, and these are the ones worth bending:

  • → Allow sound effects for ages 4–6. A roaring lion or a honking car helps them express concepts they don't have the motor skills to mime precisely.
  • → Skip the category signal (the "movie" hand gesture, etc.) unless kids already know it. Focus on acting and guessing, not the meta-signals.
  • → Give a pass option. If a child freezes, let them pass without losing a point. The goal is fun, not pressure.
  • → Double the timer for younger actors. 60 seconds instead of 30 keeps the game moving without leaving kids stranded.
  • → Let a friend stand with them. For very shy or young kids, a buddy can whisper encouragement and help them think of how to act the word.

Charades Themes for Birthday Parties

A theme narrows the guessing field, which is the real reason it helps rather than the decoration. If the room already knows every word is a sea creature, a six-year-old flapping their arms gets guessed in four seconds instead of forty. Five sets that hold up:

Dragon, Unicorn, Wizard, Fairy, Castle, Magic Wand, Enchanted Forest
