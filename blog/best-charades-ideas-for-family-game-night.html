50 Best Charades Ideas for Family Game Night in 2026

🎭

Written by the Charades Generator team. We build the free word generator this site runs on. The last time we went through the word bank by hand we cut sixty-three entries — words that read perfectly well on a list and died the moment somebody actually had to act one out. That is the bar everything here is held to.

The hardest part of family game night is rarely the rules. It is the ten minutes beforehand, when somebody is scrolling for a word list and everyone else is quietly losing interest. Below are 50 charades words in three difficulty tiers, so you can hand the easy pile to a seven-year-old and the hard pile to the uncle who takes this far too seriously — in the same game, without either of them noticing they are being managed.

A word earns a place on this list by surviving two questions: can somebody physically do it, and will somebody in the room recognise it. That sounds obvious, and it rules out a surprising number of words that look perfectly good written down. "Jealousy" is a fine word and a terrible charade. For ages roughly five to eight, and for any adult who has never done this before — which is more of them than you would expect. Everything here is one recognisable action or a shape you can draw in the air.

💡 Tip for easy rounds: Ninety seconds, not sixty. Young children spend the first half of a turn deciding what to do rather than doing it, and cutting one off mid-decision is how you end up with a child who does not want another go. Let the sound effects slide too — the silence rule is not worth a meltdown.

💡 Tip for medium rounds: Settle the sound-effects question before the first turn, not during the fourth. Either ruling is fine; changing it halfway through is what starts the arguments. The same goes for whether "close enough" counts — decide once, out loud, and let one person have the final say.

Teens and adults who want to work for it. Expect these to run the full clock, and expect at least one to fail outright. That is what a hard pile is for — a tier where everything gets guessed is not a hard tier, it is a medium tier with scarier words on it.

💡 Tip for hard rounds: Give the actor ten seconds of silent thinking time before the clock starts. It sounds like a small adjustment and it changes the entire tier. Without it, the first fifteen seconds of every hard turn is somebody standing completely still, visibly panicking.

How to Run the Perfect Family Charades Night

Mixing the tiers matters more than any individual word on the list. Three easy rounds in a row and the adults drift off; three hard ones and the children do. This is the shape we keep coming back to for a mixed-age table:

  • Start with Easy words for the first few rounds to warm everyone up and include younger players from the start.
  • Switch to Medium once the group is warmed up — usually after 10–15 minutes.
  • Save Hard words for dedicated adult-only rounds or for teams that want a real challenge.
  • Mix categories freely — going from an animal to a movie title to an action keeps the game unpredictable and fun.
  • Keep rounds under 2 minutes — longer timers slow the pace. If nobody guesses, just reveal the answer and move on.

One last thing, and it has nothing to do with the words: clear a space to act in before you start. Charades dies quietly when the actor is performing in the two-foot gap between the coffee table and the sofa. Move the table.
