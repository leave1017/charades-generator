How to print charades cards that actually come out right

Most printable card sets go wrong in the same place: the print dialog quietly shrinks the page. Browsers and printer drivers default to “Fit to page” or “Shrink oversized pages”, which scales everything down by four to eight percent to keep clear of the printer's unprintable edge. Nothing looks wrong on screen, but the cards come out small and two sets printed on different days will not match each other.

Print settings

  1. Set Scale to 100% or Actual size.
  2. Leave Margins on Default — the layout already reserves 10 mm.
  3. Untick Headers and footers so the URL and date stay off the sheet.
  4. Tick Background graphics so the difficulty dots print.
  5. Match the paper size you chose above to the paper in the tray.

Paper choice

Use 160gsm card stock or heavier. On ordinary 80gsm copy paper the word shows through from the back, and a card lying face down can be read by the other team — which quietly ruins the game.

Card stock also survives an evening of shuffling and being pulled out of a bowl. If plain paper is all you have, keep the drawn cards in a container rather than face down on the table.

What is on each card

Every card carries the word in the largest type that fits it without breaking across a line, the category in small caps at the top, and a coloured dot at the bottom for the difficulty — green for easy, amber for medium, red for hard. The dot is deliberately small: a full colour band would drain an ink cartridge across six sheets, and the dot reads just as fast from across a table.

Cut lines are shared between neighbouring cards, so a sheet of nine is four straight cuts rather than nine little boxes to trim. On A4 each finished card is 63 × 92 mm, close to a standard playing card; on US Letter it is 65 × 86 mm.

How many cards to print

Allow roughly six to eight cards per player for an evening. Eight people playing for about an hour will get through 50 to 60 cards, which is six sheets. Print a few more than you think you need — cards guessed in three seconds are used up just as fast as hard ones, and spares let you quietly drop any word that does not suit the group.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make printable charades cards?

Choose the categories you want, pick a difficulty and how many cards you need, then press Print cards. Your browser's print dialog opens and you can either send the sheets to a printer or choose Save as PDF to keep them. Each sheet holds nine cards with dashed cut lines shared between neighbours, so every cut is one straight pass across the page.

What size are the printed charades cards?

On A4 each card is 63 by 92 millimetres, close to a standard playing card. On US Letter the cards are 65 by 86 millimetres. To get those measurements you must print at 100 percent scale — if the print dialog is set to Fit to page, every card comes out several millimetres small.

What paper should I print charades cards on?

Use 160gsm card stock or heavier. On ordinary 80gsm copy paper the word shows through from the back, so a card lying face down on the table can be read by the other team. Card stock also survives being shuffled and pulled out of a bowl all evening.
